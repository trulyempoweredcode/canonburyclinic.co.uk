Laser Therapy: A Modern Approach to Pain Relief and Healing

Laser therapy at Canonbury Clinic for chronic tendon, joint and soft tissue pain in Islington, explained by osteopath Hussein alongside realistic expectations.

9 September 2026 · Laser Therapy · By Hussein Eshref

Pain that has been sitting with you for months, sometimes years, tends to bring a particular kind of frustration. You have tried rest. You have tried stretching. You may have had hands-on treatment that helped for a while but did not hold. The problem keeps coming back, and you start wondering whether anything will actually shift it. That is the situation I see regularly at Canonbury Clinic, and it is exactly where laser therapy tends to make a real difference.

I am Hussein, an osteopath with over 30 years of clinical experience. I want to explain what laser therapy is, why I use it for specific presentations, and why it has become one of the more valuable tools we have at the clinic for people dealing with persistent, stubborn soft tissue problems.

What Laser Therapy Actually Does

Low-level laser therapy, sometimes called cold laser or photobiomodulation, uses specific wavelengths of light to penetrate tissue and stimulate cellular activity at the site of injury or chronic inflammation. It is not heat treatment. The laser itself produces no burning sensation. What it does is interact with cells in a way that can accelerate tissue repair, reduce localised inflammation, and support the natural healing processes that the body has often been unable to complete on its own.

That last point matters. In chronic conditions, the body has frequently entered a holding pattern where the tissue is not fully healed but not actively inflamed either. It is stuck. Laser therapy is designed to interrupt that pattern and prompt the tissue into a more active repair phase. Whether that is a tendon that has become thickened and painful, a joint capsule that has stiffened following injury, or a muscle that has been in chronic tension for months, the same principle applies.

You can read more about how we use this approach at the clinic on our laser therapy treatment page, which outlines the conditions we find it most useful for and what a course of sessions typically involves.

Who I Tend to Recommend It For

Laser therapy is not my first line of treatment for an acute injury. If you have pulled something last week, manual osteopathic techniques are usually where I start. But for people who have been carrying a problem for three months, six months, or longer, I find that laser therapy can help in ways that hands-on work alone sometimes cannot.

Common presentations I see in Islington where laser therapy may support recovery include:

  • Tendinopathies that have not responded to standard rehabilitation
  • Chronic soft tissue injuries around the knee, hip, or elbow
  • Persistent pain around the shoulder joint or rotator cuff
  • Post-inflammatory joint stiffness
  • Repetitive strain injuries in people doing desk-based or manual work

Shoulder problems are worth mentioning specifically. The shoulder is a complex structure, and chronic pain there often involves multiple layers - muscle tension, bursal irritation, tendon involvement, and restricted joint movement. If you have been managing shoulder pain and are not getting the progress you were hoping for, our page on shoulder and arm pain treatment in Islington explains how we approach these cases, including where laser therapy fits into the overall plan.

How It Sits Within an Osteopathic Approach

I do not use laser therapy in isolation. At Canonbury Clinic, it is part of a broader treatment plan that addresses the root cause of why a problem has persisted. That means looking at how you are loading a joint, how surrounding structures are contributing to the issue, and what needs to change for the problem not to return.

Laser therapy can help reduce pain and inflammation enough to make manual work more effective. It can make a joint more accessible, or a tendon more responsive to rehabilitation loading. I think of it as clearing the path rather than doing the whole job. The work of restoring proper movement, correcting compensatory patterns, and building resilience still needs to happen alongside it.

For people in Islington who have been stuck with a chronic complaint and feel like they have exhausted the straightforward options, it is worth having a conversation about whether laser therapy is appropriate for your specific situation.

What to Expect From a Session

Sessions are straightforward. The laser device is applied directly over the affected area. You will feel no significant sensation - some people notice a very mild warmth, most notice nothing at all. A session typically lasts between ten and twenty minutes depending on the area being treated. There is no downtime, and most people return to normal activity immediately.

Results are not always immediate. Some people notice a change after two or three sessions. For longer-standing conditions, a course of six to eight sessions is more common before a clear pattern of improvement becomes apparent. I will always be honest with you about what to expect and whether I think the progress justifies continuing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is laser therapy in Islington safe?

Low-level laser therapy has a well-established safety profile when delivered by a trained clinician. It is non-invasive, produces no significant heat, and does not damage tissue. At Canonbury Clinic, we use it as part of a supervised treatment plan and assess each patient individually before recommending it.

How many sessions will I need?

This depends on the nature and duration of your problem. Acute presentations may respond in three to four sessions. Chronic or long-standing conditions often require six to eight sessions or more. I will give you an honest assessment after your initial consultation.

Can laser therapy help with tendon problems?

Tendinopathy is one of the presentations where laser therapy tends to be most useful, particularly when the tendon has not responded well to rest and conventional rehabilitation. It may support tissue repair and reduce the pain that limits your ability to load and rehabilitate the tendon properly.

Do I need a referral to access laser therapy at your clinic?

No referral is needed. You can contact Canonbury Clinic directly to book a consultation. I will assess your problem, explain what I think is driving it, and discuss whether laser therapy is appropriate for your situation.

Is laser therapy the same as shockwave therapy?

No. They are different technologies. Shockwave therapy uses acoustic pressure waves to break down calcifications and stimulate repair in dense or degenerated tissue. Laser therapy uses light energy to reduce inflammation and support cellular repair. Both are used for chronic conditions, but the indications and mechanisms differ. I will recommend the approach that suits your specific presentation.
