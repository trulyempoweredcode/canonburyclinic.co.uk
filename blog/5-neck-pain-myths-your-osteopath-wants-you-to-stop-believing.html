5 Neck Pain Myths Your Osteopath Wants You To Stop Believing

Practical guidance on neck pain causes and treatment options from an experienced Islington osteopath, for anyone with stiffness, restricted movement or pain that keeps returning.

9 September 2026 · Laser Therapy · By Hussein Eshref

Neck pain has a way of taking over your day before you even realise it. You wake up stiff, reach for something and feel that familiar sharp pull, or spend half the afternoon trying to find a position where your head does not ache. If that sounds like your experience, you are not alone, and you are probably wondering what kind of help is actually worth seeking.

I am Hussein, principal osteopath at Canonbury Clinic of Osteopathy in Islington, and I have been working with people who have neck pain for over 30 years. In that time I have seen a lot of misconceptions about what causes it, who it affects, and what osteopathic care can realistically do. This post is about clearing some of that up.

Who Actually Comes to Us With Neck Pain

The short answer is: a wide range of people. Neck pain is not a condition that belongs to one age group or one type of lifestyle. I see desk workers in their thirties whose posture has shifted gradually over years of screen time. I see tradespeople and manual workers whose necks have taken years of physical load. I see people in their fifties and sixties dealing with age-related changes in the cervical spine. And I see people who simply slept at a bad angle and cannot turn their head properly.

What unites them is that they have usually tried to wait it out, taken over-the-counter pain relief, or bought a new pillow, and the problem has either returned or never fully cleared. That is the pattern I see most often when people come through our door looking for effective support for neck pain in Angel and Islington.

Common Misconceptions I Hear Regularly

The biggest one is that neck pain is always a muscle problem. Sometimes it is, particularly in acute cases where a sudden movement or impact has strained soft tissue. But in chronic presentations, the issue often involves the joints of the cervical spine, nerve irritation, or restricted movement patterns that have built up over time. Treating only the muscle without addressing the underlying restriction rarely produces lasting relief.

Another misconception is that if an X-ray or scan has not shown anything significant, there is nothing to treat. Imaging picks up certain structural changes, but it does not capture the full picture of how your neck is actually moving, how your surrounding musculature is compensating, or where load is being distributed unevenly across the cervical joints. These are the things I am assessing when I examine you in the clinic.

A third is that osteopathy is just about manipulation. Manual therapy is one tool, and a useful one, but an osteopathic assessment looks at the whole picture: your posture, your movement patterns, the relationship between your neck, upper back, and shoulders, and how your daily habits may be contributing to the problem. The goal is to find the root cause, not mask the symptom.

What to Expect When You Come In

Your first appointment is largely about assessment. I want to understand your history, how the pain started, how it behaves across the day, what aggravates it and what eases it. I will look at how you hold your head and neck at rest, how you move through different ranges, and where restrictions or sensitivities are sitting.

From there, treatment is built around what I find. For straightforward soft tissue tension and joint restriction, hands-on work can begin to restore movement and reduce discomfort relatively quickly. For longer-standing problems, particularly those involving calcifications or chronic tissue change, we may discuss options such as shockwave therapy or laser therapy. These are not first-resort treatments, but they can help with stubborn presentations that have not responded adequately to manual work alone.

I will also give you clear guidance on what you can do between sessions. Specific exercises, posture adjustments, or modifications to how you are working or sleeping. Recovery is not just what happens in the clinic.

Why Location Matters Less Than You Think (But Still Matters)

People sometimes travel considerable distances to see a specialist. But for a musculoskeletal condition like neck pain, consistency of care makes a real difference, and attending regularly is easier when the clinic is local. We are based in Islington, easily accessible from Angel and the surrounding areas, and that proximity means people actually follow through with a course of care rather than trailing off after one session.

We also see patients who are managing other issues alongside neck pain. Lower limb problems, for instance, can influence posture in ways that feed upward into the cervical spine. If you are also dealing with joint discomfort lower down, it may be worth reading about our approach to knee pain at our Angel clinic, as these problems are sometimes connected in how the body is compensating overall.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my neck pain needs professional attention?

If your pain has persisted for more than a few weeks, is affecting your sleep or daily function, or involves symptoms like pins and needles, numbness, or headaches, speaking with an osteopath can help you understand what is going on and whether structured care may support your recovery.

Is osteopathy suitable for neck pain that has been present for years?

Yes. Chronic neck pain often responds well to a thorough osteopathic assessment and a structured treatment plan. For very long-standing presentations, we may introduce additional therapies such as shockwave or laser treatment where appropriate, as these can help with tissue-level changes that manual work alone may not fully address.

Will I need a referral to come to the clinic?

No referral is needed. You can contact us directly to book an initial assessment. We work with people who are self-referring as well as those coming through their GP or another healthcare provider.

How many sessions will I need?

This depends entirely on the individual and what the assessment reveals. Some people notice meaningful improvement within a few sessions. Others with more complex or long-standing problems benefit from a longer course of care. I will give you an honest picture after the first appointment, based on what I find.

Can neck pain be connected to other areas of the body?

Very often, yes. The neck does not exist in isolation. Shoulder mechanics, upper back stiffness, and even lower limb alignment can all influence how load is distributed through the cervical spine. A full osteopathic assessment looks at these relationships rather than treating the painful area in isolation.
