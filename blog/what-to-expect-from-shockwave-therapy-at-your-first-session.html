What to Expect From Shockwave Therapy at Your First Session

Explains who shockwave therapy may suit for chronic tendon pain, what sessions involve, and common misconceptions, for patients considering treatment in Islington.

3 November 2026 · Back Pain · By Hussein Eshref

Most people who come to see me about a persistent tendon problem or a pain that simply will not settle have already tried the standard route. They have rested it, taken anti-inflammatories, done some stretching, and waited. And yet the problem is still there, often months later, sometimes longer. If that sounds familiar, I want to be direct with you: this is exactly the kind of presentation where shockwave therapy can make a genuine difference.

At Canonbury Clinic of Osteopathy in Islington, I see a lot of chronic soft tissue and tendon conditions that have not responded to more conservative approaches. After more than 30 years working as an osteopath, I have a clear sense of when a problem needs something more targeted. Shockwave therapy is one of the tools I reach for in those cases, and in this post I want to address who it actually helps, what the experience involves, and some of the misconceptions I regularly hear.

Who Is Shockwave Therapy Actually For?

This is one of the most common questions I get asked, and it is worth answering plainly. Shockwave therapy is not a first-line treatment for acute injury. It is designed for people dealing with chronic, long-standing conditions where the tissue has failed to heal properly on its own.

The kind of presentations I find it most useful for include plantar fasciitis that has been dragging on for months, Achilles tendinopathy, calcific shoulder tendinopathy where calcium deposits are disrupting normal tissue function, tennis elbow, and patellar tendinopathy. These are all conditions where the body's natural repair process has stalled. The tissue is not acutely inflamed in the way a fresh injury would be. Instead, it has entered a chronic, degenerative state where it needs a prompt to restart the healing response.

Shockwave delivers that prompt. The acoustic energy pulses stimulate blood flow, break down calcifications, and encourage the production of collagen, which is the structural protein your tendons depend on. This is not about masking the pain. It is about addressing the underlying tissue state that is generating the pain in the first place.

If you want to understand the full picture of how shockwave therapy works and what conditions it may support, I would encourage you to read through the detail on our clinic page before booking.

What People Get Wrong About It

The word "shockwave" puts some people off immediately. They picture something intense or painful, and arrive expecting the worst. I want to clear this up.

The sensation during treatment is more accurately described as a deep pressure combined with a rhythmic impulse. Some areas are more sensitive than others, particularly over a calcification or a very tender insertion point. But the treatment is well tolerated by the large majority of people I see, and the intensity is adjusted throughout the session based on your feedback.

Another misconception is that one session will resolve the problem. Shockwave therapy typically involves a course of three to six sessions, spaced about a week apart. The tissue responds progressively, and the cumulative effect is where the real change happens. I am always honest about this with patients from the outset.

Some people also assume that because the treatment targets tendons and soft tissue, it is completely separate from the kind of musculoskeletal assessment I do as an osteopath. In reality, the two work together. I always want to understand why the tendon has been overloaded in the first place, whether that is a postural issue, a movement pattern, a biomechanical problem somewhere else in the chain. Addressing only the tendon without looking at the broader picture is how problems come back.

How We Approach It at the Clinic

When a patient comes to see me at Canonbury Clinic of Osteopathy about a chronic tendon condition, the shockwave therapy sits within a broader clinical assessment. I want to understand the full picture before recommending a course of treatment. That includes how long the problem has been present, what has been tried before, and whether there are contributing factors that need to be addressed alongside the shockwave sessions.

We serve patients across Islington and the surrounding north London area, and I see a wide range of presentations, from runners with Achilles problems to office workers with shoulder pain that has calcified over time. The common thread is that these are people who have waited long enough. They are ready to take a more targeted approach.

For patients who also present with associated neck or upper body involvement, it is worth noting that we have specific expertise in this area. You can read more about how we approach neck pain and its management at our Islington clinic, which often forms part of a broader musculoskeletal picture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is shockwave therapy painful?

Most patients describe it as a strong pressure or deep tapping sensation rather than pain. Sensitivity varies depending on the location and the condition being treated. The intensity is always adjusted to a level you can tolerate, and most people find it manageable throughout the session.

How many sessions will I need?

A typical course involves three to six sessions, usually spaced around seven days apart. Some conditions respond faster than others. I will give you a clearer indication of what to expect after the initial assessment.

Can shockwave therapy help with calcific shoulder tendinopathy?

It is one of the conditions where shockwave therapy has a well-established role. The acoustic pulses can help break down calcium deposits and stimulate the tissue's repair mechanisms. Whether it is appropriate for your specific presentation is something I would assess before recommending a course.

Will I need to rest after each session?

Some mild soreness in the treated area for 24 to 48 hours after a session is normal. I generally advise avoiding high-impact activity during that window. Beyond that, complete rest is not usually necessary, and I will give you specific guidance based on your condition.

Is shockwave therapy available in Islington?

Yes. We offer shockwave therapy at Canonbury Clinic of Osteopathy, which is based in Islington and serves patients from across the local area. You can get in touch directly to discuss whether it may be suitable for your condition.
