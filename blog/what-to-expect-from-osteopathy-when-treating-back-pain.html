What to Expect From Osteopathy When Treating Back Pain

From your first assessment to a realistic treatment plan, here is what happens when you bring back pain to our Islington clinic.

11 July 2026 · Back Pain · By Hussein Eshref
The Bigger Picture

Who Actually Gets Back Pain in Islington, and What Is Really Going On

Back pain does not discriminate. I see it every week at Canonbury Clinic - people from all walks of life in Islington walking through the door with the same frustrated look. They have tried rest, painkillers, maybe a few weeks of generic exercises from a leaflet. The pain is still there. Sometimes it is worse. What I want to do in this post is cut through the noise and talk honestly about who back pain actually affects, what is usually driving it, and what tends to make a real difference.

Who We Treat

The Types of People I See with Back Pain in Islington

Islington is a dense, active borough. People walk a lot, commute hard, sit at desks for long hours, and carry children, groceries, and laptops on bodies that are already under strain. The back pain I see here reflects that reality.

Office workers with chronic lumbar stiffness that builds through the week. Builders and tradespeople dealing with acute muscle strain or disc irritation from years of physical loading. Runners and cyclists whose back pain has started affecting their training. Parents who put their body through the demands of childcare without much thought until something locks up. Older adults in Islington managing degenerative changes in the spine that periodically flare into something they cannot ignore.

There is no single profile. But there is a common thread: most people have been managing symptoms rather than addressing the underlying cause. That is where I focus my attention.

Finding the Cause

What Is Actually Causing Your Back Pain

When someone comes to me with back pain, I am not just looking at the painful area. The lumbar spine does not function in isolation. Pain in the lower back often reflects a pattern of restriction or imbalance somewhere else in the body - tight hip flexors, poor thoracic mobility, a pelvis that has been sitting out of alignment for months or years. The back is often where the strain accumulates, not where the problem originates.

I use osteopathic assessment to understand how you move, where you are compensating, and what structures are under load. That might include soft tissue work, joint mobilisation, and specific techniques aimed at restoring normal movement mechanics. The goal is not to make you comfortable in the short term. It is to get the spine and surrounding structures working in a way that reduces the likelihood of this happening again.

If you want a fuller picture of how I approach this at the clinic, you can read more on our back pain treatment page, which covers the range of conditions we commonly see and how osteopathy can help.

Chronic Back Pain

When Back Pain Has Been Going On for Months or Years

Chronic back pain is a different problem to an acute episode. When pain has been present for months or years, the tissues involved change. You may have developed calcifications, areas of dense scar tissue, or a pattern of guarded movement that has altered your posture and gait. Standard soft tissue work still has a role, but for these longer-standing cases I often consider whether advanced therapies are appropriate.

Shockwave therapy is one option I use for chronic musculoskeletal conditions that have not responded well to other approaches. It works by delivering acoustic energy into the affected tissue, which can help stimulate cellular repair, break down calcifications, and improve blood flow to areas that have become difficult to treat by hand alone. If you have been dealing with persistent back pain and want to understand whether this might be relevant to your case, our shockwave therapy service explains how it works and what conditions it may support recovery from.

I do not recommend shockwave for every back pain presentation. It is suited to specific chronic conditions and I assess each person individually before recommending it.

Myths and Facts

Common Misconceptions About Back Pain

The first one I hear regularly is that back pain is just something you have to live with as you get older. That is not a position I accept. Age-related changes in the spine are real, but pain is not an inevitable outcome of those changes. Many people with significant degenerative findings on imaging have no pain at all. The relationship between structure and symptoms is more complex than a scan suggests.

The second misconception is that rest is the answer to acute back pain. In most cases, controlled movement is far more effective than prolonged rest. Keeping the spine mobile, within a comfortable range, supports tissue recovery and prevents the kind of guarding and stiffness that prolongs the episode.

The third is that once you have had back pain, you will always have it. I have worked with people in Islington who came in expecting to manage a permanent problem and left with tools and a treatment plan that reduced their pain significantly over time. That does not happen for everyone, but it happens far more often than people expect when the root cause is properly identified and addressed.

Common Questions

FAQs About Back Pain in Islington

How do I know if my back pain needs osteopathic treatment or something else?

If your back pain has persisted for more than a few weeks, is affecting your daily function, or keeps returning after short periods of relief, an osteopathic assessment can help identify what is driving it. I will tell you clearly if I think your presentation needs a different clinical pathway, including referral for imaging or specialist review.

Is back pain treatment at Canonbury Clinic suitable for older adults?

Yes. I regularly work with older patients in Islington managing degenerative disc changes, lumbar stenosis, and related conditions. Treatment is adapted to each person's age, health status, and specific presentation. The approach is gentle where it needs to be and targeted throughout.

Can shockwave therapy help with lower back pain specifically?

Shockwave therapy may support recovery from certain chronic soft tissue and musculoskeletal conditions affecting the back. Whether it is appropriate for your situation depends on the specific nature of your pain, how long it has been present, and what structures are involved. I assess this on an individual basis.

How many sessions does back pain treatment typically take?

This varies considerably depending on whether the problem is acute or chronic, how long it has been present, and how your body responds. Many people notice meaningful improvement within three to six sessions, but some longer-standing conditions require more sustained work. I aim to give you an honest picture of likely progress at your first appointment.

Do I need a GP referral to see an osteopath in Islington?

No. You can book directly with us at Canonbury Clinic. A referral is not required, though we are happy to communicate with your GP if that is useful for your care.
